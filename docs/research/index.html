Research in progress

What has been established, what is new since the printed edition, and what is still open.

The research behind the history is still going on. Each question below has an ID (RQ-01 and so on) used in the research files. A question is answered when the record is in hand; partly answered when some of it is; open when the right source has not yet been found or searched.

Most of the American evidence comes from New York City’s Municipal Archives, the US National Archives (passenger lists, censuses, naturalization papers and draft cards), FamilySearch and Ancestry. In Europe, the records of Kobrin, Divin and Kovel have not been indexed and are still in the archives in Belarus and Ukraine; some Podolian records are now free online.

The research questions

13 questions: 4 answered · 7 partly answered · 2 open. Select a question to see what has been found.

  • RQ-01When and how did Morris Rodilitz arrive?Answered

    Status: Answered

    He landed at Ellis Island from the Breslau, from Bremen, on 16 December 1907, aged 18, from Czerniany. His certificate of arrival, filed with his naturalization papers, confirms it.

    People: Morris Rodilitz

    Key sources: RZ-S36, RZ-S84

  • RQ-02When and how did Sylvia Hausman arrive?Open

    Status: Open

    She gave 1910 as her year of arrival. Her crossing has not been found under many spellings; she may have travelled under another name, or by way of Cuba like her mother.

    People: Sylvia Hausman (Rodilitz)

  • RQ-03When and how did Jacob and Mary Rodilitz arrive?Answered

    Status: Answered

    Jacob swore he arrived on 9 May 1907 on the Ryndam from Rotterdam; the Holland-America register and a probable passenger-list line agree (about 70% for the line). Mary and the four youngest landed from the Pretoria on 22 December 1913.

    People: Jacob Rodilitz · Mary (Miriam Reizel) Prozansky (Rodilitz)

    Key sources: RZ-S73

  • RQ-04Who was Benjamin Zaslow, and how did he come?Partly answered

    Status: Partly answered

    The Beni Zaslowsky of the Friesland (January 1904) was probably a different man. Benjamin was probably the “Berl Saslawer” of the Haverford, Liverpool to Philadelphia, March 1904, with his brothers Itzik and David (about 80–85%). His 1910 marriage licence, which names his parents, would settle it.

    People: Benjamin Zaslow

    Key sources: RZ-S25, RZ-S52

  • RQ-05Is the Zaslaw family of the Circassia, 1893, Catherine’s family?Answered

    Status: Answered

    Yes, about 90%: Basche, Jety and Beile match Bessie, Beckie and Catherine, including the Hebrew names on their gravestones. The Leib travelling with them was probably a relative, not Catherine’s father.

    People: Bessie Weiss (Saslow) · Catherine Saslow (Zaslow) · Leib Zaslaw

    Key sources: RZ-S06, RZ-S32

  • RQ-06Which towns in Europe did each line come from?Partly answered

    Status: Partly answered

    Rodilitz: Kovel (Jacob’s birthplace), probably Maciejów, then Czerniany, Kobrin, Brest and Divin. Hausman: Grodno province, probably Brest. Zaslow: Obodovka in Podolia. Saslow: Tulchin, where Benjamin was born in 1863; registered in Soroki and as colonists of Dombroveny, Bessarabia.

    People: Jacob Rodilitz · Rose Shapiro (Hausman) · Benjamin Zaslow · Benjamin Saslow

    Key sources: RZ-S102

  • RQ-07Did Sylvia’s parents come to America?Partly answered

    Status: Partly answered

    Her mother, Rose, very probably came from Havana in 1925 and died in the Bronx in 1932. Her father, Joseph, had died before 1925.

    People: Rose Shapiro (Hausman) · Joseph Hausman

    Key sources: RZ-S60, RZ-S12

  • RQ-08Who were Morris’s brothers and sisters?Answered

    Status: Answered

    Harry, Meyer, Benny, Ida, Esther, Louis and Sarah, named with their birth dates in their father’s 1927 petition; six of their marriages have been found.

    People: Harry Rodilitz · Meyer Rodilitz · Benny Rodilitz · Ida Rodilitz (Hovelin) · Esther Rodilitz (Feinberg) · Louis Rodilitz · Sarah Rodilitz (Gordon)

    Key sources: RZ-S38

  • RQ-09When did Molly Gold and the Weiss family come?Open

    Status: Open

    Bessie’s death certificate names her parents, Harry (Aharon) Weiss and Molly Gold. Their arrival, if they came, has not been traced.

    People: Molly Gold · Harry (Aharon) Weiss

    Key sources: RZ-S44

  • RQ-10What were the home towns like, and what records survive there?Partly answered

    Status: Partly answered

    The towns are described in Part one. Tulchin’s Jewish registers (1861–1906), a few early Obodovka registers and the 1875 Podolia census are free online. The Jewish records of Kobrin, Divin, Kovel and Brest are not online and are held in Belarus and Ukraine.

    Key sources: RZ-S96, RZ-S97, RZ-S102

  • RQ-11Who were the earlier generations in Europe?Partly answered

    Status: Partly answered

    Known so far: Yonah Rodilitz; Abraham and Ida Prozansky; Joseph Hausman and Rose Shapiro; David Zaslow and Katia Goldberg; Herman and Rose Lesnoff; Harry (Aharon) Weiss and Molly Gold; and David Saslow, probably Duvid son of Berko.

    People: Yonah Rodilitz · Abraham Prozansky · David Zaslow · David (Duvid) Zaslaver

    Key sources: RZ-S91, RZ-S82, RZ-S102

  • RQ-12Why did they leave when they did, and how did they travel?Partly answered

    Status: Partly answered

    No record gives any of them a reason. Part one sets their departures against the history of the time; every main route was used: Bremen, Hamburg, Rotterdam, Antwerp, Glasgow, Liverpool and Havana.

  • RQ-13What happened to the relatives and towns left behind?Partly answered

    Status: Partly answered

    Appendix B gives the history of the towns from 1914 to 1945. No individual relative who stayed has yet been traced.

Appendix E · Open questions and further research sets out the records still to get, and Records lists every source searched.

Sylvia Hausman (Rodilitz)

c. 1892–1952

AboutFamilyRecordsResearch notes

Sivia (Sylvia) Hausman, born about 1892, gave her birthplace as Grodno, Russia, and later said she came to America in 1910; her crossing has not been found. She was the daughter of Joseph Hausman and Rose Shapiro. She married Morris Rodilitz at 41 Malta Street, Brooklyn, on 7 November 1915, and they had four children, among them Theodore. In 1925 her widowed mother joined them at 537 Georgia Avenue. She died on 14 December 1952 and is buried at New Montefiore Cemetery.

Morris Rodilitz (R-010) and Sivia (Sylvia) Hausman (H-010)

Marriage certificate (RZ-S01). The record says: - Married 7 Nov 1915 at 41 Malta St, Brooklyn, by Rev. Max Mushkin(?), of 41 Malta St. - Groom: Morris Rodilitz, of 115 Malta St; age 26 (born about 1889); white; single; salesman; born Grodno, Russia; father Jacob; mother Miriam Prozansky; first marriage. - Bride: Sivia Hausman, of 312 Palmetto St; age 23 (born about 1892); white; single; born Grodno, Russia; father Joseph; mother Rose Shapiro; first marriage. - Witnesses: Isaac Levy and Fevia(?) Farber.

Morris's arrival. Ellis Island list, SS Breslau from Bremen, arrived 16 Dec 1907, line 20. The record says: Motel Rodeletz, 18, single, occupation possibly baker, reads and writes, Russian, Hebrew. Last residence Czerniany; nearest relative "mother Mariem Rodeletz, Czerniany, Gub. Grodno". Passage paid by his father; $2. Going to join "father Jankel Rodeletz, 326 Snediker Ave, Brooklyn". (Ancestry's index adds born "Czerwany", 5 ft 3 in, blond, blue eyes.) Conclusion: this is Morris (high): Motel is the Yiddish name behind Morris, and the age, year, father and mother all fit. Czerniany is probably Charniany (Chernyany, Черняны), now Malaryta district, Brest region, Belarus. Corrected in later research: before 1914 it was in Brest district (uezd), Velikorita volost, not Kobrin district, and lies about 27 km south-south-west of Kobrin (medium-high; a village of about 100 Jews before 1941).

Morris's naturalization (RZ-S43). JGSNY index to Brooklyn naturalizations (Kings County Supreme Court): "Rodolitz, Morris/Motel", declaration vol. 70 p. 330 (1912), petition vol. 220 p. 60 (about 1919–20). Brother "Harry/Hersch" two pages later (vol. 70 p. 332; petition vol. 220 p. 122). His WWI draft card says "declared intention", born "Grodna"; 1920 census "papers submitted"; 1925 New York census citizen, naturalized "Bklyn 1921". The papers themselves are imaged only on FamilySearch (blocked by its "I am human" check); a postal request to the Kings County Clerk is drafted in the the research. Sylvia became a citizen through Morris (1925 census); she filed no papers of her own.

Conclusion: Theodore's parents. The draft card names his mother as Sylvia Rodilitz. This is the only Rodilitz marriage in New York City before 1919 (searched in RZ-S03). In 1930 and 1940 Theodore is listed as a son in Morris and Sylvia's household.

Other facts from the compiler’s tree (RZ-S08): - Children: Millie (about 1917, New York), Theodore (1919), Blanche (about 1927, New York). - A fourth child (new, RZ-S19): "Baby Rodlitz", a son, born 6 Aug 1924, died 10 Aug 1924 at Unity Hospital, Brooklyn (congenital atelectasis). Father Morris Rodlitz, mother Sylvia Hausman, both born Russia; home 188 Malta St, his parents' house. Buried Mount Lebanon Cemetery, 11 Aug 1924. - Morris: in the 1930 census he gives his occupation as "Novelty" and his year of arrival as 1907. In the 1940 census he is a stock clerk. - Sylvia: gives her year of arrival as 1910 in the 1930 census. She died 14 Dec 1952 and is buried at West Babylon, Suffolk County, NY (RZ-S08.11).

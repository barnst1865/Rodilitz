Sources and notes

Archives, record collections, published works and the notes to every chapter.

This history, in its third edition, draws on research carried out in October and November 2026 for the Rodilitz–Zaslow research files, in which every source has a code (RZ-S01 and so on) and a permanent link. The notes below cite the records for each statement; Ancestry links need a subscription; NYC Municipal Archives certificates and the Rotterdam registers are free online.

Archives and record collections. NYC Municipal Archives, Historical Vital Records (certificates of birth, marriage and death, Kings and Bronx counties). National Archives (NARA): passenger lists of vessels arriving at New York (M237, T715) and Philadelphia (T840); naturalization records of the US District Courts for the Eastern Districts of New York and Pennsylvania; federal censuses 1900–1940. New York State censuses 1915 and 1925. Pennsylvania death certificates. Stadsarchief Rotterdam, Holland-America Line archive 318-04, registers of passage money. Hamburg passenger lists. Har Jehuda Cemetery burial registers. Montefiore and New Montefiore cemeteries, interment searches. FamilySearch (passenger lists, naturalization indexes, the National Homes for Disabled Volunteer Soldiers registers, WWII draft cards, California Death Index). JewishGen databases, including the Bessarabia databases of the National Archives of the Republic of Moldova. State Archives of Khmelnytskyi Region (the 1875 Jewish census of Podolia; Obodovka’s registers) and of Vinnytsia Region (Tulchin’s Jewish registers), and the Central State Archive of Ukraine (Obodovka, 1918 and 1927–28), all as free scans on Wikimedia Commons. Statue of Liberty–Ellis Island Foundation passenger database. Find a Grave. The family’s Ancestry tree (export of 22 October 2026).

Published works. Gur Alroey, “Out of the Shtetl,” Leidschrift 22:1 (2007). Jürgen Sielemann, “Eastern European Jewish Emigration via the Port of Hamburg, 1880–1914.” Aubrey Newman, “Trains and Shelters and Ships” (University of Leicester, 2000). Encyclopaedia Judaica, articles “Pale of Settlement,” “Kobrin,” “Tulchin” and “Pogroms.” Jewish Encyclopedia (1906), “Kobryn,” “Grodno” and “Podolia.” Philip Cowen, report on Russian immigration (1906–07), National Archives. Kobrin Yizkor Book and Brest-Litovsk Yizkor Book, English translations on JewishGen. Yahad-In Unum and Virtual Shtetl local histories.

How the four families connect

Theodore and Doris, their parents and grandparents, and the four families behind them.

In this chapter
  1. Spellings, names and dates

Theodore Rodilitz and Doris Zaslow took out their marriage licence in Brooklyn on 7 September 1948. He was the son of Morris Rodilitz and Sylvia Hausman of Brooklyn; she was the daughter of Benjamin Zaslow and Catherine Saslow of Philadelphia. Behind them stand four immigrant families, two on each side.

On Theodore’s side, the Rodilitzes came from the Kobrin and Brest district of Grodno province, now in Belarus, with roots in Kovel in Volhynia, now in Ukraine. Jacob Rodilitz, a Hebrew teacher and the son of Yonah, went to America first, in 1907; his wife Mary, daughter of Abraham Prozansky, and their eight children followed over the next six years. The Hausmans, Sylvia’s family, were probably from Brest-Litovsk. Sylvia gave 1910 as the year she came, and her widowed mother, Rose, very probably followed in 1925, by way of Havana.

On Doris’s side, both families came from southern Podolia, now in Vinnytsia province, Ukraine. The Saslows, her mother’s family, were probably from Tulchin, where in 1875 a boy who was very probably Benjamin Saslow was living with his father, David, a townsman of Soroki, across the Dniester in Bessarabia. Benjamin Saslow, a paperhanger, reached America in 1892, and his wife and two small daughters followed in 1893. The Zaslows, her father’s family, came from Obodovka, about 42 km from Tulchin. Benjamin Zaslow probably arrived in Philadelphia in March 1904 with two of his brothers, and his parents followed to Brooklyn in 1906.

Spellings, names and dates

The family name appears as Rodilitz, Rodlitz, Rodolitz, Rodeletz, Rodelets, Rodelec and Rudales; Rodilitz is the form Theodore used and is used here. Zaslow appears as Zaslawer, Soslawer, Saslawer, Zaslof and Zosloff, and Saslow as Saslaw, Zaslaw and Sarlow.

Everyone in these families had a Yiddish or Hebrew name as well as the English one they used in America. The records show many of them: Morris was Motel, Harry Hersch, Meyer Meier, Benny Berl, Ida Chaje, Louis Leib, Sarah Sure; Bessie Saslow was Basche (Hebrew Batya) and Catherine was Beile; Benjamin Zaslow’s Hebrew name was Dov Ber. Jacob Rodilitz was Yaakov and Mary was Miriam Reizel. These names are often the key to matching a European record with an American one.

Russia used the Julian (Old Style) calendar until 1918; it was 12 days behind the modern calendar in the 1800s and 13 days behind from 1900. Hebrew dates on gravestones follow the Jewish calendar. American records use the modern calendar.

Names that look alike. Zaslow and Saslow are different families: Doris’s father was Benjamin Zaslow and her mother was Catherine Saslow, daughter of another Benjamin, Benjamin Saslow. The names were spelled interchangeably by clerks, so each record has to be read with care. There are also three Sylvias: Theodore’s mother, Sylvia Hausman; Doris’s elder sister, Sylvia Zaslow (born 1911); and Theodore and Doris’s own daughter.

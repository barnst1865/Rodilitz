Open questions and further research

The records still to get, and the questions still open.

The most useful records still to get

  • The 1910 Philadelphia marriage licence of Benjamin Zaslow and Katie Saslow (no. 260214), held by the Philadelphia Register of Wills. It names both sets of parents and should confirm that Benjamin was the son of Jacob and Miriam Zaslawer of Brooklyn.
  • Moishe Velvel Prozansky’s veteran’s file. The Department of Veterans Affairs sends a free summary of a dead veteran’s file, including his parents’ names; his would name Mary’s parents. A request is drafted.
  • Jacob Rodilitz’s 1950 death certificate, which should give his mother’s name.
  • Photographs of Jack and Ike Zaslow’s gravestones at Montefiore, to settle whether Jack’s Hebrew name was Yaakov or Yochanan.
  • The Har Jehuda burial registers at Temple University, for the Saslow and Zaslow burials of 1936–1940.
  • Rose Hausman’s immigration file, which may explain how she entered from Cuba in 1925 and name her husband’s place and date of death.
  • Tulchin’s births of 1861–66, free online, for Berko son of Duvid Zaslaver, and its deaths of the 1880s and 1890s for the rest of the family.
  • Soroki’s Jewish family lists (revision lists) of the 1850s, in the National Archives of Moldova, which would show David Zaslaver’s whole household, his father Berko, and perhaps his wife.

Questions

  • Who was B. Rozankrans, Jacob’s brother-in-law at 70 Orchard Street in 1907, who paid his passage? Possibly a relative of Mary’s Prozansky family; an Abraham Pruzensky, born about 1867, was living in Harlem in 1905 (an unverified lead).
  • Where was the Rodilitz home before 1907: Maciejów, Czerniany, Kobrin, Divin, or several of these in turn?
  • When and how did Sylvia Hausman come to America in about 1910?
  • Who was David Saslawer, the youngest of the three brothers on the Haverford, and who was “S. Weisberg” of Amsterdam Avenue?
  • Were the two Davids related? Jacob Zaslow, born in 1855, was also a “Zaslawer” and the son of a David, and his sons used the same surname as Benjamin Saslow’s family. If the two families were related, Doris’s parents were cousins. Nothing yet shows it: Jacob’s mother was Katia Goldberg, Benjamin Saslow’s mother is not known, and Jacob was not in David Zaslaver’s household in 1875.
  • Who raised Doris after her father’s death in 1940, when she was thirteen?
  • The European records. JewishGen has no Rodilitz or Prozansky records for Kobrin, Divin or Kovel: those registers are not yet indexed. The originals are in Belarus and Ukraine; letters are drafted. The 1858 census of the Kovel district on Wikimedia Commons lists peasants only; the lists of Kovel’s Jews are on FamilySearch and still to be read, for a young Yonah Rodilitz.

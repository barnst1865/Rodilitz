In Europe

Place Connection Notes for a visit
Kobrin, Divin, Czerniany, Brest The Rodilitz and Hausman home towns Belarus. Kobrin’s Jewish history is told in the Kobrin Yizkor book, in English on JewishGen
Kovel, Maciejów (Lukiv) Jacob Rodilitz’s birthplace and home Volyn region, Ukraine
Tulchin, Obodovka, Bershad Probably the Saslows’ town (Tulchin); the Zaslows’ town (Obodovka); Ike Zaslow’s burial society (Bershad) Vinnytsia region, Ukraine
Terespol, Włodawa, Chełm The nearest places in Poland: Terespol faces Brest across the Bug, about 40 km from Czerniany; Włodawa has a preserved 18th-century synagogue complex, now a museum; Maciejów is about 40 km east of the Dorohusk crossing near Chełm Poland
Hamburg Harry (1910) and Mary with the four youngest (1913) BallinStadt emigration museum, on the site of the emigrant halls
Bremerhaven Morris (1907) German Emigration Center
Rotterdam Jacob (1907), Meyer (1911), Benny (1912) Hotel New York, the former Holland-America Line offices; Fenix migration museum
Hamburg and Glasgow Bessie Saslow and her daughters (1893)
London, Leman Street The Zaslawer brothers spent three months in London before sailing in 1904 Site of the Jews’ Temporary Shelter, Whitechapel
Liverpool Benjamin Zaslow (1904) and his parents (1906) sailed from here Pier Head; the Merseyside Maritime Museum is closed until 2028

In America

Place Connection Notes for a visit
Ellis Island Arrival of most of the family, 1893–1925 Statue of Liberty National Monument
70 Orchard St, Manhattan The address on the probable passenger-list line for Jacob, 1907 A block from the Tenement Museum, 97 Orchard St
326 Snediker Ave, 721 Vienna Ave, 115 and 188 Malta St, 41 Malta St, 537 Georgia Ave, Brooklyn The Rodilitz homes and synagogue, 1907–1940s East New York; much changed
98 Barrett St, Chester St, Brooklyn Jack and Miriam Zaslow’s homes Brownsville and East New York
Montefiore Cemetery, Springfield Gardens, Queens Jacob and Mary Rodilitz, Block 11, row 7L, graves 1–2; Jack and Miriam Zaslow, Block 32, row 13R, graves 25–26; Ike Zaslow Photographs of the Zaslow stones would show Jack’s Hebrew name
New Montefiore Cemetery, West Babylon Morris and Sylvia; Meyer, Harry and Louis, in the “Jacob & Miriam Rose Rodolitz” plot, Block 15 Long Island
Pier 53, Washington Ave, Philadelphia Benjamin Zaslow landed here in 1904 Site of the Washington Avenue immigration station; now a park
787 S. 3rd St, 2208 S. 5th St, 2163 E. Cambria St, Philadelphia The Saslow and Zaslow homes South and North Philadelphia
Har Jehuda Cemetery, Upper Darby Benjamin and Bessie Saslow; Catherine and Benjamin Zaslow; baby Mildred The cemetery’s burial registers are at Temple University’s Special Collections
Anaheim, California Theodore and Doris’s home

About this site

Who made it, the conventions it follows, and where the material comes from.

This site gathers the family history of the Rodilitz, Hausman, Saslow and Zaslow families: the families of Theodore Rodilitz (born in Brooklyn in 1919) and Doris Zaslow (born in Philadelphia in 1926), who married in Brooklyn in 1948.

Who made it

The history and this site are compiled by the Koik family. The site was last updated on 10 November 2026. The text of the history is the Fourth edition · November 2026; the site and the printed edition are updated separately, and research found since the printed edition is shown on the Research page.

Corrections and contributions

If you have a correction, a photograph, a family recollection or a document to add, please open an issue on the site’s GitHub page (a free GitHub account is needed). Say which page it concerns and, if you can, where the information comes from.

What is on the site

Its backbone is the text of the family history, also available as a printable PDF. Around it are pages for everyone found in the research, the records behind it, a family tree and a map. Each person’s page starts with a short biography taken from the history; the working research notes behind it are folded away below.

Conventions

  • Names. Everyone in these families had a Yiddish or Hebrew name as well as an English one, and clerks spelled the surnames many ways: Rodilitz, Rodlitz, Rodolitz, Rodeletz, Rodelets; Zaslow, Zaslawer, Soslawer, Saslawer; Saslow, Saslaw, Zaslaw. Headings use the form each family used in America.
  • Zaslow and Saslow are two different families: Doris’s father was a Zaslow, her mother a Saslow.
  • Places are given as the records name them, with today’s name: Kobrin (Kobryn, Belarus), Divin (Dzivin), Kovel (Ukraine), Tulchin (Tulchyn), Obodovka (Obodivka), Soroki (Soroca, Moldova).
  • Dates. Russia used the Julian (Old Style) calendar until 1918. Hebrew dates on gravestones follow the Jewish calendar. American records use the modern calendar.
  • Evidence. What a record says is kept separate from what is concluded from it. Conclusions are written as conclusions (“probably”, “about 80%”). In the family tree a dashed line means a relationship that is probable but not proven.
  • Colors. Brick red marks Theodore’s side (the Rodilitz and Hausman families) and blue Doris’s side (the Zaslow and Saslow families); walnut and ochre belong to the site itself. Family names are always given in words as well.
  • Codes. Every source has a permanent code (for example RZ-S01), every person an ID (for example R-010 or Z-011), and every research question an ID (for example RQ-04). The codes match the research files.

Privacy

People born after 1930 who may still be living are named only where the history itself names them, and have no pages of their own. Identity numbers are left out. The research does not record living descendants.

How to cite

Give the page title, “The Rodilitz and Zaslow Families, compiled by the Koik family”, the address of the page, and the date you consulted it.

How this site is made

The pages are generated from the family-history text and the research files by a small script, so that the site can be rebuilt whenever the research moves on. The source is kept on GitHub.
